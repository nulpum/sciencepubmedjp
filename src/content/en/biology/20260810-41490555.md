## The key finding

A comprehensive 2026 review spanning research from 2015 to 2025 has clarified the botanical and chemical differences among three traditional Chinese medicinal plants collectively known as Muxiang. Researchers identified 429 distinct compounds across Aucklandia lappa (Mu-Xiang), Vladimiria souliei (Chuan-Mu-Xiang), and Inula helenium (Tu-Mu-Xiang) — all perennial members of the Asteraceae family whose dried roots have been used interchangeably in traditional medicine. Among these compounds, 149 are sesquiterpene lactones (SLs), the primary bioactive constituents responsible for various therapeutic effects. The review demonstrates that despite their morphological similarities, these three plants possess distinct chemical profiles that correlate with different pharmacological activities.

## What the study looked like

This was a comprehensive literature review analyzing research published between January 2015 and September 2025. Researchers systematically searched four major scientific databases: Web of Science, PubMed, ScienceDirect, and the Chinese database CNKI. They examined studies covering ethnomedicinal uses, chemical composition (phytochemistry), quality control methods, and pharmacological properties of the three Muxiang varieties. The review synthesized findings from hundreds of studies that employed various analytical techniques including high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS), DNA barcoding, and hyperspectral imaging combined with machine learning algorithms. This multi-method approach allowed researchers to build a comprehensive picture of how these plants differ at molecular and genetic levels, despite their confusingly similar appearances.

## Why researchers think this happened

The historical confusion stems from the plants' morphological similarities and overlapping traditional uses in Chinese medicine. All three species are perennial plants from the same botanical family with similar-looking dried roots, making visual identification challenging. According to the review, the similarities between A. lappa and V. souliei are particularly pronounced. However, modern phytochemical analysis reveals that while all three plants contain sesquiterpene lactones as their main bioactive compounds, the specific types and concentrations vary significantly. The review suggests these chemical differences likely arose through different evolutionary pathways and growing environments. The identified sesquiterpene lactones demonstrate various biological activities including anticancer, anti-inflammatory, gastrointestinal protective, antimicrobial, liver protective, and neuroprotective effects. The presence of additional compound classes — monoterpenoids, triterpenoids, phenylpropanoids, and flavonoids — in varying proportions further distinguishes the three varieties.

## How to read this carefully

This is a review paper synthesizing existing research rather than a new experimental study, meaning it summarizes findings from other studies conducted over a decade. While the identification of 429 compounds is impressive, the review does not specify how many individual studies contributed to this total or their quality. The pharmacological activities mentioned are associations observed in laboratory and animal studies, not confirmed clinical effects in humans. The review acknowledges that "future validation of distinctions is essential," indicating that more research is needed to confirm these differences in real-world medical applications. Additionally, the studies reviewed likely varied in methodology, sample sizes, and quality, which the review format does not allow us to assess individually. Readers should understand that traditional use does not equal proven efficacy, and laboratory findings don't automatically translate to safe or effective human treatments.

## What this means for everyday life

For practitioners of traditional Chinese medicine and patients who use these remedies, this research highlights an important quality control issue: what you think you're taking might not be what you're actually getting. The development of DNA barcoding and machine learning techniques to distinguish these plants suggests that accurate identification is now more feasible, potentially improving consistency in traditional medicine products. If you're interested in herbal medicine, this review underscores why standardization and quality control matter — morphologically similar plants can have meaningfully different chemical compositions. For the scientifically curious, this research demonstrates how modern analytical chemistry can validate and refine traditional knowledge, separating folklore from measurable biochemical reality. Given these findings, anyone using Muxiang varieties might want to ask suppliers about quality control methods and seek products that use modern authentication techniques to ensure they're receiving the intended variety.
