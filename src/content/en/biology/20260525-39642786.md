A systematic review of 38 studies revealed that pregnant women with higher body weights frequently encounter weight stigma throughout their maternity care. Researchers identified five key themes showing these women face repeated shame, humiliation, and harmful preconceptions from healthcare providers. The stigma is linked to weight-related conversations, additional interventions, and restrictions on birthplace choices that current guidance doesn't acknowledge as potentially stigmatizing. However, the analysis found that individualized, supportive care from healthcare professionals may help alleviate these negative experiences. The researchers suggest adopting a "shame-sensitive lens" within culturally safe maternity care could transform support for these women, moving away from judgmental interactions that currently define many higher-weight patients' experiences with prenatal and birth services.
