Researchers are uncovering how gut bacteria and their metabolites interact with the immune system during cancer treatment. These microbial communities appear to influence immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) effectiveness by priming dendritic cells, enhancing T-cell infiltration into tumors, and reshaping the tumor microenvironment. Imbalances in the microbiome (dysbiosis) are also linked to immune-related side effects from these therapies. Early-stage studies of fecal microbiota transplantation, engineered probiotics, and dietary changes show potential to improve ICI response and reduce adverse events, though multicenter trials are needed to confirm safety and efficacy. This emerging field suggests that analyzing a patient's unique microbiome alongside other factors could eventually help personalize cancer immunotherapy—a step toward precision oncology that accounts for the trillions of microbial partners we carry.
