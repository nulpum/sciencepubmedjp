A scoping review analyzed 17 clinical trials from 3,291 records to examine whether *Lactobacillus* probiotics affect mood in humans. Single-strain interventions, particularly high-dose *L. plantarum* administered for ≥8 weeks, were associated with improvements in anxiety, sleep quality, and inflammatory biomarkers. Multi-species formulations showed reductions in depressive symptoms and changes in neurobiological markers. However, researchers noted substantial variation in strains, dosages, durations, and outcome measures across studies, making direct comparisons difficult. While *Lactobacillus* interventions show promising mood-modulating potential, especially with prolonged use, the evidence remains inconsistent. Standardized protocols and more rigorous studies are needed before firm recommendations can be made about using these probiotics for mood support.
