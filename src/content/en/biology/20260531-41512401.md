Land degradation in India affects a vast area roughly equivalent to 1.2 million square kilometers, caused primarily by water erosion, wind erosion, soil salinity, and mining activities. This degradation leads to reduced soil quality, biodiversity loss, and compromised crop yields. However, a comprehensive review of rehabilitation strategies shows promising results. Sub-surface drainage systems increased cropping intensity by 25-100%, while agroforestry and bench terracing enhanced soil organic carbon and reduced erosion by up to 80%. These context-specific interventions—including shelterbelts and bio-engineering measures—are linked to significant improvements in soil health and agricultural productivity. The findings suggest that implementing appropriate rehabilitation measures across diverse agro-ecological zones may be essential for restoring degraded lands and addressing livelihood and environmental challenges in affected regions.
