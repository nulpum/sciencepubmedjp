Comparative endocrinology reveals that the insulin superfamily is one of the oldest hormonal systems in animal evolution. Researchers have identified homologous insulin-like hormones in primitive creatures such as sponges and hydra—organisms that emerged hundreds of millions of years ago. By studying how this hormone family evolved from invertebrates to vertebrates, scientists gain deeper insights into its ancestral functions. This evolutionary perspective helps researchers understand not just ancient biology, but also modern conditions linked to insulin, such as metabolic syndrome and insulin resistance in humans. The approach shows that studying hormones across species—from the simplest metazoans to mammals—can illuminate the original roles of signaling molecules that now regulate metabolism, growth, and homeostasis in complex organisms.
