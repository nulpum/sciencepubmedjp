Researchers analyzed data from 58,229 participants across 19 studies to assess the global prevalence of Hikikomori—a condition characterized by extreme social isolation. The pooled prevalence was **8.0%** (95% CI, 4.9%-12.9%), with no significant differences between East Asia and Western regions, or between pre- and post-COVID-19 pandemic periods.

Interestingly, prevalence estimates varied dramatically based on assessment method: studies using the 25-item Hikikomori Questionnaire reported rates of **21.7%**, while other questionnaires found just **5.0%**. Studies using nonprobability sampling methods also reported higher rates (12.5%) compared to probability sampling (3.1%). Older age and lower study quality were linked to higher prevalence estimates. The findings suggest Hikikomori is a widespread global phenomenon requiring standardized diagnostic tools for accurate assessment.
