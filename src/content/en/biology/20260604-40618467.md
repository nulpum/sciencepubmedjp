Scientists recently discovered a second major group (clade II) of these critical enzymes, which appears widespread across diverse environments. Early evidence suggests the two clades may differ in key traits—clade II organisms have been observed reducing N₂O at pH < 5.0, making them the only known acidophilic N₂O reducers. Researchers also see hints of differences in substrate affinity and oxygen tolerance between clades. However, experts caution that these binary classifications rest on limited data and lack mechanistic understanding, risking oversimplification. A deeper grasp of how biochemistry links to ecology is needed to accurately harness these microbes and mitigate atmospheric N₂O.
