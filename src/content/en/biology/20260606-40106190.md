Celiac disease (CeD) currently has only one approved preventive approach: a gluten-free diet, which often falls short of complete symptom control. Researchers are now exploring *Lactiplantibacillus plantarum* (LP), a probiotic bacterium, for its potential role in managing CeD triggers. According to a recent review, LP supplementation is associated with several beneficial effects: it may improve the ratio of intestinal villus height to crypt depth (maintaining gut structure), modulate gut microbiota composition, enhance tight junction proteins that strengthen the intestinal barrier, and increase digestibility of problematic gliadin fragments. LP also shows links to regulation of CD4+ T cells, immune cells involved in CeD. While these findings suggest LP could minimize gastrointestinal symptoms in CeD patients, the review emphasizes that significant clinical and nonclinical studies are still needed before therapeutic use can be recommended.
