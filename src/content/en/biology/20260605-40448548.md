When viruses infect cells, they don't just hijack the usual suspects—they also exploit R-loops, peculiar structures where RNA sticks to one DNA strand, displacing the other. These three-stranded tangles form naturally during gene activity, but viruses have learned to use them as guides. Evidence shows R-loops influence where viral DNA integrates into our chromosomes, help regulate latent (dormant) viral genomes, and even trigger production of small interfering RNAs that keep mobile viral elements in check. Understanding this interplay could open new antiviral strategies: researchers propose mapping R-loop hotspots in infected cells and developing drugs that either stabilize or disrupt these structures, potentially blocking viral integration or forcing latent viruses out of hiding for targeted elimination.
