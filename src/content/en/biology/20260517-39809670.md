Recent computational and experimental breakthroughs have revealed tiny proteins called microproteins, produced by diverse microbial species living in and on our bodies. These molecules, which were hidden from traditional detection methods, represent a promising frontier in the search for new antimicrobial agents. As antibiotic resistance continues to threaten modern medicine, researchers are turning to the human microbiome—the trillions of microorganisms that inhabit our bodies—as a potential source of novel treatments. The identification of these microproteins opens new avenues for understanding how our resident microbes interact and compete with each other, potentially providing insights that could be translated into therapeutic applications against drug-resistant infections.
