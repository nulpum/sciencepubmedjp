Once confined to laboratory genetics, *Arabidopsis thaliana* has become a powerful tool for studying how organisms adapt to their environments. Through the 1001 Genomes Project and 73 outdoor common garden experiments conducted over 15 years, researchers have cataloged how this small plant's DNA changes across its global range. By comparing genomes from populations living in contrasting climates, scientists are mapping which genes are linked to survival traits like flowering time and stress tolerance. This real-world genetic data may help predict how species respond to climate change—not by engineering solutions, but by understanding the natural genetic variation that allows some populations to thrive while others struggle in shifting conditions.
