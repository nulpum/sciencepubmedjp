Trace amines, including tyramine and β-phenylethylamine, are endogenous substances recently recognized as neurotransmitters in the brain. They act through trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs), part of the G protein-coupled receptor family, expressed across multiple brain regions. TAARs modulate neurotransmission, neuronal excitability, cognition, mood, locomotion, and even smell. Disrupted trace amine circuits have been linked to schizophrenia, Parkinson's disease, addiction, depression, and anxiety. In rodents, dysregulated TAAR signaling is associated with altered dopamine and serotonin systems—key pathways implicated in psychiatric conditions. Now, zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) are emerging as a powerful translational model for studying trace amine neurobiology, complementing rodent research. These small fish enable pharmacological and genetic experiments to unravel how trace amines influence CNS processes and brain disorders, offering new insights into potential therapeutic targets.
