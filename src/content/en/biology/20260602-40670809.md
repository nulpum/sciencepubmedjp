A review examines how the microbiome-gut-brain axis shapes early neural development through bidirectional communication between gut bacteria and the central nervous system. Microbial metabolites, neurotransmitters, and gut hormones influence brain function, while the brain modulates gut activity via the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis and autonomic nervous system.

Factors affecting an infant's gut microbiome—including mode of birth, gestational length, breastfeeding, and antibiotic exposure—may impact neurodevelopment. Gut dysbiosis has been linked to conditions such as ADHD and autism spectrum disorder in children. Researchers are exploring therapeutic approaches including probiotics, prebiotics, fecal microbiota transplant, and even CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing to support optimal neurodevelopment, though these remain investigational.
