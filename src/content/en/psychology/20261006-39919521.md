## The key finding

Researchers developed the first evidence-based framework specifically designed to integrate equity considerations into public health emergency dashboards. Called DIRE (Dashboard Instrument to Review Equity), this three-layered tool emerged from analyzing 102 studies and COVID-19 response gaps. The framework structures decision-making across three layers—context, data flow, and dashboard users—and organizes information into three buckets: data sources, knowledge pillars, and interventions. This represents the first systematic attempt to ensure that emergency response tools guide officials toward both rapid and equitable choices when time and data are scarce.

## What the study looked like

This was a scoping review and tool development study conducted in 2025. Researchers followed PRISMA-ScR guidelines to systematically search databases including PubMed and Scopus, ultimately analyzing 102 articles. They performed three levels of thematic analysis to identify patterns across the literature. The team also examined existing COVID-19 dashboards to understand how decision support tools functioned during the pandemic. Tool development involved building a conceptual model based on the DIKW Pyramid (Data-Information-Knowledge-Wisdom) and an Informatics Stack—frameworks from information science that describe how raw data becomes actionable guidance. The resulting DIRE framework synthesized findings across five major themes that emerged from the literature.

## Why researchers think this happened

The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical weaknesses in public health decision-making infrastructure. Officials faced urgent choices with incomplete data and no structured guidance for weighing trade-offs—particularly trade-offs affecting vulnerable communities differently. Five themes emerged from the literature review: COVID-19's impacts, health equity principles, emergency decision processes, dashboard design, and existing frameworks. The researchers concluded that no existing tool adequately combined speed with equity considerations. By grounding DIRE in the DIKW model, they created a pathway from raw data collection through information processing to knowledge synthesis and ultimately to wise interventions. The three-layer structure addresses context (understanding the emergency's nature), data flow (moving from sources to actionable insights), and end users (ensuring dashboards serve actual decision-makers' needs).

## How to read this carefully

This study describes a framework, not an evaluation of its real-world performance. DIRE has not yet been tested during an actual public health emergency, so we don't know how well it functions under pressure or whether it measurably improves equity outcomes. The framework emerged from literature review rather than field trials. The 102 articles analyzed were heavily weighted toward COVID-19 experiences, which may not represent all emergency types. The authors explicitly note that further research is recommended—DIRE is a first-generation tool requiring validation. Additionally, "equity" itself involves complex, context-dependent judgments that a framework can guide but not fully automate.

## What this means for everyday life

For anyone who wondered why pandemic responses seemed to hit some communities harder than others, this work acknowledges that gap and attempts to address it systematically. The next time a public health emergency unfolds—whether infectious disease, environmental disaster, or other crisis—officials using DIRE-informed dashboards might better balance speed with fairness. This could mean faster identification of which neighborhoods need resources, clearer communication of trade-offs, or more consistent consideration of how interventions affect different groups. While the framework won't eliminate difficult choices, it offers a structured approach to making those choices visible and defensible. For communities historically overlooked in emergency responses, tools like DIRE represent acknowledgment that equity requires intentional design, not just good intentions.
