A meta-analysis of 37 studies involving 251 effect sizes found a small but consistent positive association (r = 0.18) between motor competence and executive functions in children and adolescents aged 5-18 years. The link held across different study types: cross-sectional (r = 0.17), longitudinal (r = 0.15), and experimental (r = 0.25). All motor skill categories—including locomotor abilities like running, object control like catching, and stability skills like balancing—showed associations with cognitive functions such as inhibition, working memory, and cognitive flexibility. The relationship remained consistent regardless of age. While experimental studies suggest that motor skill interventions may benefit executive functions, researchers emphasize more studies are needed to understand the mechanisms behind this brain-body connection.
