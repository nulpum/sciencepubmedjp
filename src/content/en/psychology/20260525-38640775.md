A systematic review of 50 studies examined how mental imagery functions in people with generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) and chronic worry. Among 27 studies examining imagery patterns, researchers found that high worriers consistently showed heightened negative and worry-related mental imagery alongside diminished ability to imagine positive future scenarios. Importantly, their overall capacity to generate mental images remained unimpaired—a finding that may have treatment implications. The review also analyzed 23 intervention studies testing imagery-based approaches for worry and GAD, which showed mixed results regarding effectiveness. Even imaginal exposure as a standalone technique produced inconsistent outcomes. These findings suggest that while the *content* of mental imagery is altered in chronic worriers (more negative, less positive), the underlying imagery system still functions normally, potentially offering a pathway for developing new therapeutic approaches.
