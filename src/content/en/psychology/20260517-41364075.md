Researchers conducted an exhaustive analysis of physiology education studies from 1990 to August 2025, revealing that educators face six recurring obstacles: engaging large classes, explaining abstract concepts, preventing fragmented knowledge, teaching system-level complexity, ensuring preclass preparation, and providing formative feedback. Rather than requiring complete curriculum overhaul, the review demonstrates that targeted interventions—such as interactive polling, peer instruction, scaffolded problem-solving, and low-stakes assessment—can incrementally improve learning outcomes. Each strategy is explicitly grounded in established learning theories including constructivism, cognitive load theory, dual coding, and retrieval practice. The analysis provides physiology instructors with an actionable, theory-informed toolkit for enhancing teaching effectiveness through manageable, step-by-step modifications to existing courses.
