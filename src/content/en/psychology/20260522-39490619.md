Large retrospective studies consistently link regular exercise to reduced dementia risk, and animal research has identified potential mechanisms for how physical activity may protect the brain. However, a review of randomized controlled trials reveals a critical detail: aerobic exercise shows stronger and more consistent cognitive improvements in patients with mild cognitive impairment compared to those with Alzheimer's disease. This suggests a **narrow window of opportunity** for exercise interventions—starting earlier in the disease process appears key. Among various exercise types studied, aerobic exercise produced the most benefits, improving multiple cognitive domains and enhancing patients' ability to live independently. The wide variance in study methods may explain inconsistent outcomes across trials, but the pattern is clear: **prevention beats late-stage intervention**.
