Dual-process theory divides thinking into System 1 (fast, pattern-based) and System 2 (slow, analytical). Since the late 1970s, many emergency medicine educators have taught residents that most diagnostic mistakes stem from System 1's cognitive biases, urging them to engage System 2 as a safeguard. However, a literature review reveals this "check-and-balance" framework lacks research support. An alternative view suggests both systems work harmoniously, with errors arising from gaps in underlying knowledge rather than flawed thinking styles. This means drilling analytical strategies may not fix diagnostic accuracy—instead, building experiential knowledge through deliberate practice could be more effective. The stakes are high: accurate emergency diagnosis can be life-or-death, making evidence-based teaching critical.
