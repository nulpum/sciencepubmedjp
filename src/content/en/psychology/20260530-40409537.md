Researchers conducted a hybrid concept analysis combining literature review with interviews of 10 community workers and 15 residents to define health empowerment in Chinese cultural context. They identified five key attributes: community participation, policy support, resource use, cooperation, and self-care decision-making. The study found that health empowerment requires antecedents like social support, resource mobilization, and health knowledge sharing. When successful, it's linked to improved personal health, increased self-care awareness, and stronger community belonging. Unlike Western models focusing mainly on individual autonomy, the Chinese framework emphasizes reciprocal interaction between community members and organizations, promoting both individual health management and collective community cohesion simultaneously.
