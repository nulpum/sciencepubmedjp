Researchers analyzed 22 studies involving 1,074 patients with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or Alzheimer's disease (AD) who received transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS)—a technique that applies weak electrical currents to the scalp. When combined with standard treatments like medication or cognitive exercises, tDCS was associated with improved scores on multiple cognitive tests (MMSE, MoCA, MODA) and faster brain response times (reduced P300 latency) compared to control groups receiving only standard care or sham stimulation (all P < 0.05). While these findings suggest tDCS may benefit global cognition in MCI and AD patients, the technique remains investigational and should not replace established medical care. More research is needed to determine optimal protocols and long-term effects.
