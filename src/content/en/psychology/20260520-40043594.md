A 2025 review in the European Journal of Surgical Oncology explores how understanding human factors—elements like fatigue, decision-making processes, team dynamics, and situation awareness—can reduce surgical mistakes. The authors, drawing from British NHS experience, emphasize that even skilled surgeons are fallible humans who make errors regularly. Some surgeons mistakenly believe they can operate for countless hours without breaks, ignoring basic human limitations. The review advocates for a "just culture" approach, where surgical incidents are investigated to understand *why* something happened rather than simply assigning blame. They also highlight how burnout can negatively impact surgical error rates and patient safety, underscoring that recognizing human limitations isn't weakness—it's essential for safer surgical oncology practice.
