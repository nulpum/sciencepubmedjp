A systematic review analyzed 14 randomized trials from an initial pool of 703 studies to examine how virtual reality (VR) training compares to conventional teaching in dental education. The findings suggest that VR dental simulators are linked to improvements in students' knowledge and practical skills. Notably, adding haptic technology—which provides tactile feedback—was associated with enhanced hand skills, theoretical knowledge, self-confidence, and overall learning experience. The controlled, safe environment of VR allows students to practice dental procedures without patient risk. However, researchers emphasize that more studies are needed on cost-effectiveness, student satisfaction, and potential drawbacks before VR becomes a standard educational tool in dentistry.
