Researchers analyzing old versus new items that participants cannot consciously distinguish often conclude unconscious processing occurred when behavioral or neural differences emerge. However, this contrast methodology faces two critical problems: Kelley's Paradox (a statistical phenomenon from regression to the mean with measurement error) and unequal strength skewness between target and non-target items in Signal Detection Theory. Through computational simulations, formal analysis, and a recognition memory experiment, scientists demonstrated these artifacts can create apparent unconscious effects even when none exist. The findings suggest many published conclusions about unconscious perception and cognition may be invalid. A narrative literature review revealed this flawed approach remains prevalent across the field, highlighting the need for alternative analytical methods when studying mental processes below conscious awareness.
