Mental imagery-based interventions show promise for attenuating conditioned fear in healthy individuals. A systematic review examined 15 experimental studies testing two approaches: imaginal extinction (mentally visualizing feared stimuli without negative outcomes) and imagery rescripting (mentally rewriting traumatic scenarios). Both techniques were linked to reduced fear responses when compared to standard fear conditioning paradigms. Notably, imaginal extinction appeared comparable in effectiveness to traditional real-world exposure extinction in several studies. The 13 original articles meeting strict inclusion criteria—requiring differential fear conditioning paradigms and imagery-based interventions—revealed consistent benefits. However, researchers noted that relatively few studies have explored the underlying neural and psychological mechanisms. The field would benefit from standardized intervention protocols to improve experimental control and enable better comparison across studies, helping researchers understand exactly how mental imagery modulates learned fear responses.
