Collective intelligence (CI) is when groups outperform individuals at tasks like decision-making and problem-solving. Researchers propose 5 types of CI mechanisms, from basic self-organized coordination to complex cultural learning. Comparing rats and cockroaches reveals how different paths lead to success: cockroaches achieve collective behaviors through ancestral, less cognitively complex mechanisms, while rats display primate-level traits like distributed decision-making and empathy. Both species thrive as ecological generalists, yet their "group smarts" operate on entirely different cognitive levels. This comparison helps scientists identify which features of collective intelligence are universal properties versus those requiring specific cognitive adaptations. The framework clarifies how similar group outcomes can emerge from vastly different underlying mechanisms across the animal kingdom.
