A meta-analysis of 19 randomized controlled trials involving 1,670 menopausal women found that mindfulness-based interventions were associated with improvements across seven health measures. Compared to control groups, women practicing mindfulness showed benefits for menopausal symptoms, quality of life, sleep quality, anxiety, depressive symptoms, stress levels, and mindfulness awareness. The interventions had a low dropout rate (6%) and high adherence (79%), suggesting women found them acceptable. However, researchers rated the overall evidence quality as low to moderate due to methodological limitations and small sample sizes. The studies, published between 2011 and 2024, suggest mindfulness training may offer a multi-faceted approach for managing the menopause transition, though more rigorous trials with longer follow-up periods are needed to confirm these associations.
