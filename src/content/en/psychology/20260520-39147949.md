Traditional screen-based eye tracking measures gaze patterns only in laboratory settings, but mobile eye tracking (MET) captures a participant's perspective during active behavior in real-world contexts. Recent technological advances now allow researchers to study egocentric vision across the entire lifespan, including infants. While MET provides spatially and temporally sensitive measures of visual attention that reveal how people process information during natural interactions, challenges remain in collecting, processing, and analyzing this data. A 2024 practical guide in *Behavior Research Methods* outlines technical procedures for implementing MET studies and shares open-source programs for quality inspection and visualization, aiming to make this powerful research tool more accessible to psychological scientists studying attention patterns in cognitive and socioemotional functioning.
